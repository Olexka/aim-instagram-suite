# AIM Expert Conversion Content

Create content that gives useful understanding, establishes professional differentiation, and leads naturally to a relevant service without pressure, fear, invented proof, or giving away an entire professional protocol.

## Non-negotiable workflow

Do not write the complete content immediately, even if the user supplies a topic.

### Stage 1: clarify

Before drafting, ask the user to answer or confirm all six points:

1. Which account is this for?
2. Which product or service should it lead to?
3. What problem has the audience already tried to solve alone?
4. What have they tried, and why did it not produce a stable result?
5. Which real cases, observations, facts, qualifications, or results may be used?
6. What action should the reader take after the content?

Keep the questions compact and grouped in one message. Never invent missing answers. If the conversation already contains an answer, state the inferred answer briefly and ask the user to confirm it.

### Stage 2: concept for approval

After receiving the answers, propose only:

- main meaning;
- angle;
- hook;
- format;
- structure;
- intended CTA.

Wait for explicit approval or corrections. Do not write the complete text at this stage.

### Stage 3: full content

After approval, write the complete piece using the framework below. Preserve the user's approved wording and facts. If a late change materially affects the concept, return to Stage 2 for that point.

## Content framework

1. **Recognition.** Show a concrete situation through behavior, thoughts, bodily reactions, or visible results.
2. **Partial solution.** Name what the person has already tried: reading, tests, exercises, meditation, ready-made formulas, one-off answers, or symptom correction.
3. **Limit of self-work.** Explain fairly why it brought insight, temporary relief, or no durable change. Do not claim self-work is always useless.
4. **Hidden complexity.** Show that the expert evaluates a combination of history, beliefs, behavior, body, emotional background, family patterns, context, risks, constraints, and change over time.
5. **Expert solution.** Explain how this expert distinguishes similar states, identifies the source, selects and adjusts the method, connects layers, follows the response, and translates insight into action.
6. **Proof.** Use only real anonymized cases, practice observations, work stages, behavioral change, client results, qualifications, or a genuine method. Never fabricate reviews, statistics, research, diagnoses, or outcomes.
7. **Next step.** Invite the reader to the logical continuation: diagnosis, consultation, analysis, support, training, booking, subscription, comment, or save.

## Required reasoning layer

The content must answer more than “Why is this happening?” It must also make clear:

- why knowing the cause has not yet changed the situation;
- which layer is difficult to see from inside one's own pattern;
- where professional judgment becomes necessary;
- what this specific expert does differently.

Useful formulations include:

- “Этого может быть достаточно для первого понимания”.
- “Сложность начинается, когда сценарий повторяется”.
- “Общая рекомендация не учитывает личную причину”.
- “Один и тот же симптом может возникать по разным причинам”.
- “Без диагностики человек часто работает с тем слоем, который уже видит”.
- “Понимание не всегда превращается в новое поведение”.

Adapt wording to the account's natural voice; do not repeat these phrases mechanically.

## Boundaries

- Give enough mechanism for recognition and informed choice, but do not publish the entire individualized diagnostic or intervention protocol.
- Do not manufacture scarcity, fear, dependency, or a false claim that only the expert can help.
- Do not devalue education or self-help. State precisely where general information stops accounting for the individual case.
- Keep the expert, their real process, and the reader's next decision central. Do not turn the piece into a generic encyclopedia entry.
- Match the requested account. Never apply Sergey’s context to Olga’s account, or vice versa.
